# Minimum biclique cover

Cover the 1-entries of a 0/1 matrix with the fewest all-ones rectangles and prove the count optimal with a packing witness: 1-entries no two of which lie in a common all-ones rectangle. One all-ones rectangle can contain at most one packing entry, so the packing is a lower bound on every cover; when the cover and the packing have the same size the optimum is pinned directly by the answer. Minimum biclique cover is NP-hard, and the difficulty of the unseen instances is that the minimum must be attained exactly - carrying the packing witness to the same size - rather than covering each locally visible region with one rectangle.

## Difficulty

Development instances are small matrices (at most 150 rows and 150 columns) made of a few visibly separated all-ones blocks. The minimum of each equals the block count: one rectangle per block covers the matrix and one corner cell per block packs the same count, so every greedy construction reaches the minimum.

The unseen instances (288 x 288 with 73133 ones, 576 x 576 with 292675 ones, and 864 x 864 with 659307 ones) are dense planted matrices. Each carries $ k $ rectangles with pairwise incomparable row sets and column sets, one private corner column per rectangle, and one corner row per rectangle that joins only rectangles of smaller construction index; the remaining rows and columns are shared among 40-55 percent of the rectangles, and the row and column labels are permuted. Every pair of rectangles overlaps (min pairwise row-set intersection 38, 77 and 125 rows for the three instances, up to 117, 241 and 357), so the union is dense (73133 of 82944, 292675 of 331776 and 659307 of 746496 cells). The corner cells are pairwise not co-coverable - for $ i < j $ the crossing $ (r_i, c_j) $ is never a 1-entry - so no rectangle cover can use fewer than $ k $ rectangles, while the $ k $ planted rectangles cover the matrix: the optimum is exactly $ k $, certified two-sidedly by construction. The contract declares the family's shape - planted incomparable rectangles, shared rows and columns, minimum equal to the planted count - but not the corner-column recovery or the owner-set elimination order, so the structural route has to be discovered rather than transcribed from the brief.

The maximum is *not* the locally visible structure. Measured on the shipped unseen instances:

- The closure-greedy cover route (one maximal-rectangle closure per column, then greedy set cover) reaches 65, 129 and 193 against the minima 64, 128 and 192, and an exact set-cover integer programme over the same candidates reaches the minima (0.9 s, 7.5 s and 29.1 s on the three instances); on its own the cover side is therefore not the wall.
- Exact packing formulations do not close the witness either: a structure-aware integer programme over the recovered rectangles (one row per rectangle, one constraint per overlapping pair; 35391 binaries and 8128 pair constraints at k=128) was still running after 17 minutes and 64 CPU-minutes and was stopped.
- Every measured packing search falls short of the equal-size witness: uniform restarts fail in 200 restarts, minimum-owner selection in 200, conflict-driven selection in 40, union-avoiding selection in 200 and conflict repair in 60, on every one of the three instances; the only measured route to the witness is recovering the planted ownership structure itself (owner-set peeling assigns every rectangle a row whose remaining owners are exhausted except that rectangle; a dynamic minimum-owner selection over the same owner sets also reaches the witness).

## Reference solution

`solution/biclique_cover_reference.py` recovers the cover from the minimal distinct column profiles of the matrix - the row sets of columns that contain no other column's row set - closes each profile over the columns, derives the packing witness from an owner-set peeling order (a row whose remaining owners are exactly one rectangle is assigned to that rectangle; every rectangle handled earlier owns none of the later rows, so no two chosen entries are crossed), and re-verifies covering completeness, the all-ones property and the packing condition against the raw 1-entries before writing. It solves the unseen split in under two seconds.

`tests/data/independent_certificate/producing_solver.py` is the independently executed producing implementation. It groups columns into classes of equal row sets as frozensets, reduces the classes by inclusion, derives the witness with a queue-style owner scan, re-checks both sides with set arithmetic, and also emits `optimum.csv`. It reaches the same minima in under a minute.

## Verification

The verifier re-derives every requirement from the input: each rectangle's listed cells must all be 1-entries, every listed rectangle must be non-empty with distinct indices in range, every 1-entry must be covered, the packing entries must be distinct 1-entries no two of which lie in a common all-ones rectangle (two entries $ (u_1, v_1) $ and $ (u_2, v_2) $ are co-coverable exactly when $ (u_1, v_2) $ and $ (u_2, v_1) $ are both 1-entries), the two lists must have equal length, and the common size must equal the independently certified minimum from `optimum.csv`. With equal sizes the packing proves the lower bound in-check, so a certificate that is not minimal fails even before a candidate is graded. Malformed JSON, duplicate keys, CSV quoting, wrong optima, oversized covers, uncovered entries, short packings and non-packing witnesses are covered by regression tests.

Candidate answers are read link-proof: the grader `lstat`s the candidate path, rejects symbolic links and non-regular files outright, opens with `O_NOFOLLOW`, re-checks the opened descriptor with `fstat`, and requires the realpath to stay inside the expected output directory, so an output `solution.json` that is a symlink to a packaged certificate is rejected instead of followed. The harness additionally screens each case's output directory after its invocation and records every symlink or special file in the case status; any such entry is a hard failure, while additional regular files and real subdirectories are permitted, and `test_invocation_isolation_properties` verifies the screening, the scratch wipe and the process cleanup from the shipped files. The export also enforces the contract's single-file self-containment requirement: a sibling beside the solver source is removed at staging, the submission directory is left traverse-only and the staged source root-readable only, and `test_submission_is_self_contained` asserts that state. A derivation regression test re-runs the candidate through the same sandboxed invocation path (unprivileged user, network block, resource limits, fresh scratch, and the prior invocations' runtime tree wiped first) on an additional packaged instance set that is never shown at development time, and requires a valid minimal certificate for it: a solver that replays memorised development answers, or any precomputed variant of them, cannot answer the additional set and fails it. The graded public call stages a fresh small instance set that is not the development copy, so replaying answers computed for the development instances cannot pass it; every graded invocation therefore requires its own input to be solved.

A second derivation test (`tests/test_certificate.py::test_candidate_derives_answers_from_generated_input`) generates small planted instances at verification time - the same construction with its two-sided certificate asserted before the candidate runs - and requires a valid minimal answer for each through the same sandboxed invocation path. The generated instances are a deterministic function of the submitted solver's bytes (the export records the staged source's SHA-256), so each submission always faces the same two instances while different submissions face different ones - no precomputed table or input fingerprint can answer them. Instances are deterministic synthetic constructions generated for this task; no public benchmark instances are reused. The packaged certificate is produced by the independent implementation described above, revalidated by the same grading code, and the two solvers agree on every size. Negative controls (no output, development answers only, an omniscient replay of the packaged public, unseen and derivation answers, a public-call replay of the development answers, network attempt, scratch escape, output symlinked at a certificate, uncovered entry, oversized cover, short packing, non-packing witness, malformed JSON, wrong schema, stale output) and a positive control (the reference logic with an incidental subdirectory next to its answer) were each run against these frozen bytes and rejected or accepted on their intended check; the control battery is author-side, outside the shipped image, and the shipped tests enforce the same requirements the controls probe. The 3,600-second per-invocation wall cutoff is sized against shipped methods rather than tuned tight: the reference solves the unseen split in under two seconds and the producer in under a minute, so the cutoff leaves an order-of-magnitude margin for slower legitimate methods, while the combined 14,400-second wall-clock limit is enforced over all five invocations by `test_aggregate_wall_clock_budget_is_enforced`.

## Budget

`metadata.expert_time_estimate_hours` is 48 hours for a domain specialist to reconstruct the planted rectangle structure, implement the exact cover and the matching packing witness for both solvers, and validate the construction independently. The 18000-second (5-hour) agent budget is above one tenth of that estimate (4.8 hours): it covers implementing and debugging a solver, not the authoring and independent-certificate work included in the estimate.
